# Best Tools to Scope a Software Project and Hire Developers in 2026

A fitness studio asks two developers for a quote on a booking app. One prices a class calendar and a booking form. The other adds payments, refunds, and a staff dashboard.

Both quotes can be reasonable. They cover different work.

Before you hire, write down what someone should be able to do on launch day. For the studio, that might be as simple as finding a class, booking a spot, and cancelling online. Once those basics are clear, it gets easier to see which kind of help you need.

## Turn a rough idea into a scope

Start by talking through what happens when a class fills up, or someone cancels. Does the studio need a waitlist on day one? Can staff keep taking payments at the front desk? These decisions affect what developers estimate.

### Turnfeat for scoping a rough idea

[Turnfeat](https://turnfeat.ai/) is a good fit if you want to turn a brief into work you can assign. It uses AI to organize a brief into modules, tasks, requirements, and early estimates. For the studio, that could mean separate pieces for the schedule, member bookings, and staff tools.

Turnfeat matches specialists by module. Its public profile describes contracts and escrow-backed milestone payments. If separate people build the schedule and cancellations, agree on who will test that a cancelled booking frees the spot. Confirm current fees before hiring.

### thoughtbot for discovery and development

If you are still figuring out what the problem is, thoughtbot offers a different kind of help. A discovery engagement could reveal that the studio’s booking system already works and staff mainly need a better way to manage the waitlist.

thoughtbot offers product strategy, design, and development. Ask what you will have at the end of discovery and how the build would be priced. That gives you a point to decide whether to continue.

## Use the scope to hire

Once the studio knows what belongs in the first release, it can give developers the same brief. It can ask for a calendar, online booking, and cancellations, with payments left for later.

### Upwork for comparing project bids

Upwork fits when you want several people to price that brief. You can review proposals from freelancers and agencies and hire on an hourly or fixed-price contract. Client fees apply. When comparing bids, check who will test the app, deploy it, and hand it over.

### Toptal for hiring a screened developer

Toptal fits when you already have a team and need to fill a particular role. If the studio’s team can build the booking flow but needs someone to handle checkout and refunds, it can ask Toptal to match it with a screened developer. Toptal lists a monthly fee for clients who proceed with matching. Talent costs depend on the engagement.

| What you need                                      | Where to start                          | What to ask before paying                                      |

| A scope you can hire for by module                 | [Turnfeat](https://turnfeat.ai/)        | Who will connect and test the modules?                         |
| Help deciding what to build and a team to build it | thoughtbot                              | What will discovery deliver, and how will the build be priced? |
| Several bids for a written brief                   | Upwork                                  | Which deliverables does each bid include?                      |

## What to check before accepting a quote

Before you accept a quote, write down what is included, what can wait, and how you will tell if the work is done. One check for the studio might be that when a member cancels, someone else can book the spot. That is specific enough for a developer to estimate and for the studio to verify.
